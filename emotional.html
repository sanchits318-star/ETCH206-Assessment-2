COMPONENT 07

Fostering emotional development

Emotional development moves from reliance on trusted adults for co-regulation towards emerging emotional expression, recognition and early self-regulation. Secure relationships and predictable, respectful routines support children’s wellbeing and confidence.

DOMAIN OVERVIEW

Emotional development moves from reliance on trusted adults for co-regulation towards emerging emotional expression, recognition and early self-regulation. Secure relationships and predictable, respectful routines support children’s wellbeing and confidence.

ART · MOVEMENT · MUSIC
EDUCATOR PRACTICE

Teaching competencies

✦

Educators notice and respond calmly to emotional cues, validate feelings without judgement, offer comfort and maintain predictable routines. They understand that infants and toddlers need adult co-regulation and should not be expected to manage intense feelings independently.

AUTHENTIC CURRICULUM

Learning across curriculum areas

Art: offer safe, non-toxic sensory art experiences as ways to explore colour, texture and expression. Movement and Music: use gentle lullabies, scarves and changes in tempo to support shared regulation. Participation is always optional and responsive to the child.

ORIGINAL LEARNING OPPORTUNITIES

Three experiences, thoughtfully adapted by age

Each experience is designed around the child’s stage, interests, cues and individual capabilities.

0–12 months

Heart-to-Heart Silk Scarf Sway

Support secure-base relationships and calm, responsive interaction.

Use a light, breathable scarf only as an educator-held prop, kept well away from the infant’s face, neck and sleep space. Hum softly and respond to the infant’s cues. Never leave fabric within an infant’s reach unsupervised.

EYLF Outcomes 1 & 3 · Trusting relationships
12–24 months

Mirror Emotion Play

Build early emotional vocabulary and expressive agency.

Use a shatterproof mirror and age-safe, non-toxic materials where appropriate. Model simple expressions and words such as happy, sad, surprised and calm. Follow the child’s lead and validate their own expression.

EYLF Outcomes 1 & 3 · Identity
2–3 years

Calm-Down Sensory Bottles

Explore settling strategies with a trusted adult.

Use securely sealed, robust sensory bottles that are checked before every use. Invite children to watch the contents settle while an educator models slow breathing and names body cues. This is a shared co-regulation activity, not a substitute for adult support.

EYLF Outcome 3 · Wellbeing
NOMINATED LEARNING MATERIAL

Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star (traditional) and If You’re Happy and You Know It (traditional)

MEDIA

A gentle lullaby can support familiar settling routines. An action song offers a playful way to notice and express feelings through movement.

Video 3 · Lullaby; Video 4 · Action song

Use the material respectfully and adapt your delivery to children’s ages, interests, language backgrounds and responses.

Practice reminder

Learning experiences must be voluntary, closely supervised and adapted to each child. Check every material for age suitability, choking hazards, allergens, hygiene and other risks before use. Follow service policies and family information.
