RELATIONAL & SLOW PEDAGOGY

COMPONENT 02

Teaching infants and toddlers

High-quality infant and toddler teaching is intentional, responsive and relationship-based. Educators teach through everyday interactions, careful observation, play and care routines rather than relying on formal lessons or rushed schedules.

✿
CORE APPROACHES

Presence is a teaching practice

⏳

Slow pedagogy

Resist unnecessary time pressure. Allow children to repeat, revisit, pause and deepen their exploration at their own pace.

🤲

Primary caregiving

Consistent educator relationships support familiarity, secure attachment, communication and continuity between home and the service.

👀

Intentional observation

Notice gestures, gaze, vocalisations, movement, interests and rhythms. Use what you learn to make thoughtful curriculum decisions.

CARE IS CURRICULUM

Learning lives in everyday routines

Nappy changes, mealtimes and sleep transitions are meaningful curriculum moments when educators slow down and engage responsively (Bussey & Hill, 2017).

Nappy change as relational dialogue

Announce the transition before lifting. Maintain warm eye contact, narrate each step gently and pause for the child’s response. Invite participation where developmentally appropriate, such as holding a clean nappy or climbing the change steps with support.

EYLF Outcomes 1, 3 & 5

Mealtimes as shared learning

Make mealtimes calm and social. Respond to hunger and fullness cues, use language to describe food and actions, and support children to participate at their own pace. Respect family food practices and individual needs.

EYLF Outcomes 1, 3 & 5

Sleep transitions as co-regulation

Follow each child’s sleep cues and familiar settling practices. Use predictable, reassuring language and a calm environment. Maintain safe-sleep practices and active supervision in line with current service policy and guidance.

EYLF Outcomes 1 & 3

Educator reflection

Am I moving at the child’s pace? Have I explained what I am doing? Did I leave enough time for the child to respond, participate or communicate “no”?
