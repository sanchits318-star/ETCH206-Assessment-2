LANGUAGE & LITERACY · DRAMA · PUPPETRY
EDUCATOR PRACTICE

Teaching competencies

✦

Educators use serve-and-return interaction, responsive pauses, clear language, dialogic reading and meaningful repetition. They follow the child’s focus, expand emerging words and partner with families to include home languages and communication approaches.

AUTHENTIC CURRICULUM

Learning across curriculum areas

Language and Literacy: use tactile story sacks, illustrated song cards, board books and chunky crayons. Drama and Puppetry: invite puppet conversations and familiar pretend scenarios. Technology can support communication when it is purposeful, interactive and age-appropriate.

ORIGINAL LEARNING OPPORTUNITIES

Three experiences, thoughtfully adapted by age

Each experience is designed around the child’s stage, interests, cues and individual capabilities.

0–12 months

Serve-and-Return Babble Echoes

Strengthen early communication, listening and reciprocal vocal interaction.

Share a high-contrast cloth or board book and a soft sound-making toy. Mirror the infant’s coos and babbles, pause, respond warmly and name objects that the infant is looking at. Keep the interaction face-to-face and unhurried.

EYLF Outcome 5 · Non-verbal turn-taking
12–24 months

Mystery Story Sack

Expand receptive and expressive vocabulary and connect words with objects.

Place familiar, safe objects such as a large wooden spoon, hat or soft toy in a fabric sack. Invite the toddler to choose an object, name it and explore it. Expand single words naturally, without pressuring the child to repeat.

EYLF Outcome 5 · Symbol systems
2–3 years

Chalk Story-Map Walk

Encourage emergent mark-making, narrative and meaning-making.

Offer chunky, non-toxic outdoor chalk on a suitable surface and picture prompts. Invite children to make marks and tell a story about them. Write down children’s dictated words and read their stories back to them.

EYLF Outcome 5 · Making meaning
NOMINATED LEARNING MATERIAL

Incy Wincy Spider (traditional) and Row, Row, Row Your Boat (traditional)

MEDIA

Fingerplay supports sound patterns, sequencing and gesture. A shared rowing song encourages rhythmic interaction, action words and reciprocal participation.

Use the material respectfully and adapt your delivery to children’s ages, interests, language backgrounds and responses.

Practice reminder

Learning experiences must be voluntary, closely supervised and adapted to each child. Check every material for age suitability, choking hazards, allergens, hygiene and other risks before use. Follow service policies and family information.
