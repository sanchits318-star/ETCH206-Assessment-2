DOMAIN OVERVIEW

Cognitive development across birth to three years includes early memory, object permanence, cause-and-effect reasoning, spatial awareness, curiosity and problem-solving. Infants and toddlers develop these capabilities through embodied exploration, repetition, relationships and play.

EDUCATOR PRACTICE

Teaching competencies

✦

Educators observe repeated play schemas such as enclosing, transporting and trajectory. They provide time for trial and error, avoid rescuing too quickly, and introduce language that helps children notice relationships, quantity, space and cause and effect.

AUTHENTIC CURRICULUM

Learning across curriculum areas

Mathematics: compare sizes and quantities with nesting bowls. Science: explore floating and sinking with safe, supervised water play. Engineering: test gentle ramps and large rolling objects. Integrated STEAM: investigate light, shadow and natural loose parts.

ORIGINAL LEARNING OPPORTUNITIES

Three experiences, thoughtfully adapted by age

Each experience is designed around the child’s stage, interests, cues and individual capabilities.

0–12 months

Peek-a-Boo Treasure Basket

Foster emerging object permanence and sustained visual tracking.

Use cotton muslin cloths and large, safe timber rings or other age-appropriate objects. Partially cover an object, pause, and invite the infant to rediscover it. Remain close and ensure every item is too large to present a choking risk.

EYLF Outcome 4 · Curiosity and persistence
12–24 months

Tip, Pour and Sink Water Lab

Investigate cause and effect, volume and early buoyancy concepts.

Offer a shallow, closely supervised water tray, large cups and natural sponges. Model words such as full, empty, heavy and sink. Never leave a child unattended around water; empty the tray immediately after use.

EYLF Outcome 4 · Hypothesising
2–3 years

Loose-Parts Sort and Gutter Ramp

Practise attribute classification and spatial engineering.

Provide large pinecones, seedpods, blocks and stable, low ramps. Invite children to sort by features and predict what may happen when the ramp height changes. Check all materials for age suitability and hazards.

NOMINATED LEARNING MATERIAL

Where Is the Green Sheep? (Fox & Horacek, 2004)

BOOK

The repetitive text, contrasting features and visual clues support prediction, visual discrimination, memory and early reasoning.

Video Recording 1 · Picture book reading

Use the material respectfully and adapt your delivery to children’s ages, interests, language backgrounds and responses.

Practice reminder

Learning experiences must be voluntary, closely supervised and adapted to each child. Check every material for age suitability, choking hazards, allergens, hygiene and other risks before use. Follow service policies and family information.
