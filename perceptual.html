Perceptual development and the five senses

Perceptual development integrates visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory and gustatory experiences with vestibular processing (balance and spatial orientation) and proprioception (body and movement awareness). Sensory experiences should be safe, accessible, gradual and responsive to individual preferences.

DOMAIN OVERVIEW

Perceptual development integrates visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory and gustatory experiences with vestibular processing (balance and spatial orientation) and proprioception (body and movement awareness). Sensory experiences should be safe, accessible, gradual and responsive to individual preferences.

TECHNOLOGIES · INTEGRATED STEM / STEAM
EDUCATOR PRACTICE

Teaching competencies

✦

Educators notice signs of sensory interest, discomfort and overload. They adjust the intensity and duration of experiences, supervise closely, check materials for hazards and use language that helps children describe sensory qualities.

AUTHENTIC CURRICULUM

Learning across curriculum areas

Technologies: explore low-glare light panels, simple switch-activated sound resources and oversized magnifiers with close supervision. Integrated STEM/STEAM: create safe texture pathways and compare natural and manufactured materials. Avoid small loose parts and potential allergens for children under three.

ORIGINAL LEARNING OPPORTUNITIES

Three experiences, thoughtfully adapted by age

Each experience is designed around the child’s stage, interests, cues and individual capabilities.

0–12 months

Botanical Tactile Discovery Mat

Explore tactile and visual differences through safe, supervised sensory play.

Offer a securely assembled floor mat with firmly attached, washable textures such as corduroy, fleece and smooth fabric. Stay within arm’s reach. Do not offer loose botanicals, small parts or materials that may be mouthed or inhaled.

EYLF Outcomes 3 & 4 · Sensory awareness
12–24 months

Sensory Stepping Trail

Explore balance, body awareness and different surface sensations.

Set out low, stable, non-slip trays with securely contained surfaces such as turf, cork and damp sand. Offer hand support if wanted and keep the path short and clear. Inspect for splinters, choking hazards and temperature risks.

EYLF Outcomes 3 & 4 · Balance
2–3 years

Light, Colour and Sound Lab

Explore visual discrimination, auditory matching and early comparison.

Use a low-glare light panel and large translucent tiles, plus securely sealed sound containers. Invite children to match colours and compare sounds. Check all items for breakage, heat, batteries and small-part risks.

EYLF Outcomes 4 & 5 · Exploration
NOMINATED LEARNING MATERIAL

Sleeping Bunnies (traditional movement game)

MEDIA

The game invites children to listen for a cue, pause, then move. It can support auditory attention, body awareness, balance and coordinated movement. Adapt the activity to each child’s mobility and comfort.

Video Recording 7 · Movement game

Use the material respectfully and adapt your delivery to children’s ages, interests, language backgrounds and responses.

Practice reminder

Learning experiences must be voluntary, closely supervised and adapted to each child. Check every material for age suitability, choking hazards, allergens, hygiene and other risks before use. Follow service policies and family information.
